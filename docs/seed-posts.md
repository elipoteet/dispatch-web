## 1 — Thesis · $NVDA

The best post in this batch. A genuine beat-and-raise that the market shrugged at.

> Nvidia just put up one of the largest beats I've ever seen and the stock is basically where
> it started. Revenue $96.22B, up 106% year over year, against a $92.17B consensus. Data
> center did $89B of that, up 117%. Q3 guided to $108B versus $104.2B expected. And Huang put
> a preliminary number on fiscal 2028 — roughly 70% revenue growth, against a 44% analyst
> consensus — and called it *supply-constrained*, which means the ceiling is what they can
> physically build, not what anyone will buy.
>
> The stock closed at $217.55 on Friday, down 4.6% on the day, and only about 4% above where
> it traded before the print.
>
> Here's what I think that's telling us. On trailing earnings NVDA is at 27x. On forward
> earnings it's at 18x. An 18x forward multiple on 70% guided growth is not a stock the market
> believes. The market is pricing the guidance as optimistic, or it's pricing something after
> 2028 that the guidance doesn't cover. Those are different problems and I can't yet tell
> which one this is — but "expensive" is no longer the argument against it.

**Change my mind:** Evidence the 70% figure is demand-pulled-forward rather than real — a
hyperscaler trimming committed capex, or the supply constraint easing without the revenue
following it.

**Sources:** [Nvidia Q2 FY2027 earnings — Webull](https://www.webull.com/blog/304-Nvidia-Q2-FY2027-Earnings-Beats-Revenue-EPS-Estimates-Guides-Q3-to-108B), [NVDA overview — stockanalysis.com](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/nvda/)

## 2 — Thesis · $ANET

Fills an empty ticker page, and it's a name you've actually done the work on.

> If you believe Nvidia's fiscal 2028 number, you have to have a view on what moves the data
> between those chips. Arista did over $3B in Q2, up 37.7% year over year, trailing revenue up
> 32.6%. It trades at 62x trailing, 42x forward, at $195 with a 52-week range of $114 to $215.
>
> That is a much more expensive stock than Nvidia on forward earnings — 42x versus 18x — for
> materially slower growth. The bull case has to be that networking is a longer-duration
> position in the buildout than the accelerator itself: chips get replaced on a two-year
> cycle, the switching fabric around them doesn't.
>
> I'm not sure I believe that's worth double the multiple. Posting it because I want someone
> to tell me what I'm missing on the durability argument.

**Change my mind:** A credible case that hyperscalers move meaningfully to in-house or
white-box switching — that's the whole thesis, and I don't think I've stress-tested it.

**Sources:** [ANET overview — stockanalysis.com](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/anet/)

## 4 — Question · $NVDA

Questions are the post type most likely to get a reply, and the site needs replies more than
it needs takes.

> Real question about Nvidia's quarter. Data center split into hyperscale at $48.7B and
> "AI clouds, industrial and enterprise" at $40.3B, and the second bucket grew faster — 138%
> versus 102%.
>
> Is that the healthy read or the worrying one? It could mean demand is broadening past four
> giant customers, which would make the whole thing more durable. Or it could mean a lot of
> revenue is now going to neoclouds financing purchases with debt, which is a very different
> risk. Does anyone know how to tell those apart from outside?

**Sources:** [Nvidia Q2 FY2027 segment detail — Webull](https://www.webull.com/blog/304-Nvidia-Q2-FY2027-Earnings-Beats-Revenue-EPS-Estimates-Guides-Q3-to-108B)
