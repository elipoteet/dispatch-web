# Legal Drafts and Community Guidelines

August 2026. Drafts for the disclaimer, community guidelines, terms, and privacy policy.

**Read this first.** These are drafts written to be useful, not legal advice, and nobody
involved in writing them is a lawyer. The disclaimer and guidelines are close to usable as
written because they are mostly product decisions expressed in plain language. The terms and
privacy policy are skeletons that get you thinking clearly and should be reviewed by an
actual attorney before the site is open to strangers. A section at the end lists the
specific things worth paying someone to look at.

---

## 1. The disclaimer

### Short version, for the footer of every page

> Dispatch Social is a place for students to discuss markets. Nothing here is investment
> advice. Posts are written by users, not by professionals, and are not checked for
> accuracy.

### Full version, for `/disclaimer`

**Dispatch Social is educational. It is not investment advice.**

Everything published on Dispatch Social is for information and discussion. It is not a
recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any security, and it should not be treated as the
basis for any financial decision.

**We are not advisers, brokers, or professionals.**

Dispatch Social is not a registered investment adviser, a broker-dealer, or a financial
institution of any kind. It does not manage money, execute trades, hold customer assets, or
connect to brokerage accounts. It has no relationship with any company discussed on it.

**The people posting are students.**

Content on Dispatch Social is written by verified college students and alumni. They are not
professionals, they are frequently wrong, and being wrong in public is part of what the site
is for. Nothing anyone writes here has been reviewed, verified, or endorsed by Dispatch Social.

**Market data may be wrong, delayed, or missing.**

Prices and financial figures come from third-party providers, are not guaranteed to be
accurate or current, and are shown as of the moment a post was written rather than as of
right now. Do not rely on them for anything that matters.

**Authors may hold positions in what they discuss.**

Anyone posting about a specific security is asked to state whether they hold a position in
it. That statement is self-reported and not verified. Assume that any author may own, or may
be about to buy or sell, anything they write about.

**Do your own work.**

Investing involves risk, including the loss of principal. Past performance says nothing
about future results. Before making any financial decision, do your own research and
consider speaking with a licensed financial professional who knows your situation.

## 2. Community guidelines

These are the rules of the place, written in the site's own voice and short enough that
people might actually read them. They belong at `/guidelines` and should be linked from the
composer.

**Post under your real name and your real school.** That is the whole premise. One account
per person. Do not impersonate anyone, and do not create an account for someone else.

**Say whether you own it.** When you post about a specific ticker, mark whether you hold a
position. It takes one tap, nobody else in retail research does it, and it is the difference
between an argument and an advertisement.

**Make an argument, not an announcement.** "NVDA to the moon" is not a post. What you think,
why you think it, and what would change your mind is a post. Length is not the standard;
reasoning is.

**Push back on the idea, not the person.** Disagreement is the most valuable thing that
happens here and it requires a reason. Attacking someone personally, rather than their
argument, is the one thing that gets a post removed quickly.

**Do not pump.** Coordinated promotion, hyping a position you are about to sell, or posting
about microcaps to move them will get the account removed permanently and without discussion.

**Do not post other people's private information.** Yours or anyone else's. No contact
details, no addresses, no screenshots of private conversations.

**No promotion.** No affiliate links, no paid courses, no Discord invites, no newsletters
you profit from. If you are here to sell something, you are in the wrong place.

**Nothing illegal.** No insider information, no market manipulation, no securities fraud. If
you learned something because of your job or an internship and you are not supposed to share
it, do not share it here.

**Questions are always welcome.** Not knowing something is not a failure of standards, it is
the reason this place exists. Anyone treating a genuine question with contempt is the
problem, not the person who asked.

### Enforcement

Enforcement is by hand and it is a person, not a system. In order: a note, then removal of a
post, then suspension, then a permanent removal. Impersonation, pumping, and doxxing skip
straight to the end.

Accounts removed for cause do not get their school back. Because verification runs on a
school email and one account per address, the practical effect of a permanent removal is
real, which is a large part of why the identity system is worth its cost.

## 3. Terms of service, draft

To be reviewed by an attorney. Structure and plain-language intent are here; the
enforceability is not something this document can promise.

**Who can use Dispatch Social.** Reading is open to anyone. Posting requires a verified account,
which requires an email address at a recognized school. You must be at least 18, or the age
of majority where you live, to create an account.

**Your account.** One per person. You are responsible for what happens under it. Do not share
credentials. We can suspend or remove accounts that break the community guidelines.

**Your content.** You keep ownership of everything you write. By posting you grant Dispatch
Social a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free licence to display, distribute, and archive
it on the platform, including after you delete it in the limited form described below.

**Deleting content.** You can edit a post for twelve hours after publishing and delete it at
any time. Deleting removes the text but leaves a marker so that replies from other people
remain readable and conversations do not silently rewrite themselves. Replies you did not
write belong to the people who wrote them.

**Content is public.** Dispatch Social is readable without an account and is indexed by search
engines. Anything you post publicly should be treated as permanently public, including on
your profile page. Only your email address is never displayed.

**What we do not promise.** The service is provided as is. We do not guarantee uptime,
accuracy of market data, accuracy of anything a user writes, or that the service will
continue to exist. We are not liable for financial losses arising from anything you read
here. See the disclaimer.

**Other people's content.** Dispatch Social hosts user-generated content and does not review it
before publication. We are not responsible for it and do not endorse it. Report anything that
breaks the guidelines.

**Changes.** These terms can change. Material changes will be announced on the site.

**Ending it.** You can delete your account at any time. We can terminate accounts that break
the guidelines or the law.

## 4. Privacy policy, draft

To be reviewed by an attorney, particularly on the student-data and minors questions.

**What is collected.** Your school email address, used only to verify your school and recover
your account. Your name, school, graduation year, and anything else you choose to put on your
profile. Everything you post. Your watchlist. Basic technical logs.

**What is public.** Your name, school badge, graduation year, beat, posts, replies, public
watchlist entries, and anything optional you added such as a LinkedIn link or a "now at"
line. All of it is visible to anyone on the internet and indexed by search engines.

**What is never public.** Your email address. It is not displayed, not searchable, and not
sold or shared with anyone.

**Who else touches the data.** The site runs on Vercel and stores data in Supabase, and
market data comes from third-party providers. Those providers process data on our behalf.
Nothing is sold to advertisers and there is no advertising on the platform.

**Email we send you.** Account and verification email always. Anything else is described in
the notification settings and can be turned off there.

**Your rights.** You can see everything on your profile, edit it, and delete your account. On
deletion your email address and profile are removed. Your posts follow the deletion rule in
the terms: text is removed, markers stay so other people's replies survive.

**Cookies.** Only what is needed to keep you signed in. No advertising or cross-site tracking.

**Minors.** Accounts are for people 18 and over. Some college freshmen are 17, which is a real
edge case and one of the reasons this document needs a lawyer.

**Changes.** Material changes will be announced on the site.

## 5. What is actually worth paying a lawyer for

Most of the above is either product decisions or boilerplate. These five are not.

**The 17-year-old freshman.** Plenty of first-year students are 17 for part of the year. Any
service collecting personal data from minors sits in a different regulatory position, and the
honest options are to require 18, to handle it properly, or to knowingly ignore it. Pick
deliberately rather than by accident.

**Whether anything here counts as investment advice.** The intent is clearly educational and
the platform takes no fees, holds no assets, and makes no recommendations. That is a good
position. It is still worth one professional read, because the line matters more once there
is an audience.

**Liability for user content.** Section 230 in the United States generally protects platforms
hosting user speech, but the protections have edges, particularly around content the platform
itself promotes. From the desk is hand-curated promotion by the operator, which is worth
asking about specifically.

**Student data and school email addresses.** Verifying against university domains and storing
that association is worth a sanity check, especially as more schools are added.

**The name.** A trademark search on Dispatch Social in the financial-media space, before it is
worth anything to anyone.

### Cheap ways to do this well

A student legal clinic at a law school is often free and used to exactly this kind of
question. UNH has a law school. Many universities also run entrepreneurship programs with
counsel attached, and a founder-friendly template set reviewed for an hour beats a template
set never reviewed at all.
